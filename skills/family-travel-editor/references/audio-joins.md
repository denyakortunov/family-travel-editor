# Sound continuity between shots

Inspect the actual stereo export at sound boundaries, including nested timelines and prepared mixes. A picture cut does not necessarily cut the sound; zero native fade length does not prove an abrupt edge if the fade is already in the source file.

Classify each audible boundary before editing:

- **Continuous speech or atmosphere:** preserve the bed across the picture cut. Do not fade every shot to silence.
- **Different recordings of the same place:** overlap suitable real ambience handles where needed, preserving complete words, breaths and reactions. A very short anti-click fade does not fix a conspicuous change in background texture.
- **New place or time:** let the previous atmosphere leave and the new one enter deliberately. A fade through silence can be appropriate for a chapter break; it is not a substitute for an overlapping ambience join inside continuous action.
- **Music entrance or exit:** retain the intended attack, development and ending. Start from a useful phrase; do not let a long build finally arrive just as a monologue interrupts it. If the source lacks coverage, choose another musical section or shorten the montage instead of filling with repeated pictures.
- **An intentional pause or accent:** preserve its purpose. Do not fill every silence or suppress every transient automatically.

Choose overlap length and curve for the material, not a universal frame count. Equal-power curves can maintain energy for unrelated backgrounds; complementary equal-gain curves can avoid excess gain on correlated signals. Inspect the midpoint for a dip or swell and check mono compatibility. Protect meaningful sound and speech before extending a fade.

If voices need a lower music bed, keep that bed steady through the complete speech episode rather than raising it in every pause. An embedded piano performance can motivate a related instrumental continuation, but retain scene-specific choice; one successful genre is not a rule for the whole film.

Compare processed and original sound at matched loudness. If removing a shout or noise creates artificial holes or pumping, reconsider the cut or retain more natural source sound. Do not apply aggressive isolation or an automix preset merely because the editor provides it. Review the music stem and full mix separately when diagnosing a level drop; clip presence does not establish audibility.

For changed joins, record sources, actual overlap interval, curve/level choice and reason. Check the exported transition region for chopped words, clicks, relative background jumps, dips, stereo/mono behavior and sync. ASR, VAD and loudness measurements locate concerns but do not replace listening. Use [checks](checks.md) for evidence limits and [Fairlight](fairlight.md) for the optional Resolve workflow.
