# Story and context

Catalogue all sources before treating any transcript as the whole trip. Note coverage gaps, non-speech action and the distinction between camera time and travel time. Never silently correct dates or move originals.

Keep a context ledger: fact, source, confidence, conflicting evidence, question. Use photo GPS and chosen booking messages to reconstruct places and travel order. A booking proves an intended itinerary, not that an event happened. Camera clocks and drone metadata may be wrong. Ask the author specific questions paired with short memory clips: what were you trying to do, what changed, what mattered, how did it end?

For each scene record location, setup, action, reaction/result, ending and transition. Review before/after the candidate interval. Preserve the full selected speech turn and a natural tail; camera-truncated speech should not be patched with invented words. Avoid two attractive shots that repeat the same information unless the repetition has an explicit story purpose.

Narration adds personal context rather than describing everything visible. Mark uncertain lines as questions, collect the complete recording script and timing estimates, then retain the author's accepted takes. Never describe generated memories as autobiographical truth.
