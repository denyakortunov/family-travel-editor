# Story and context

Catalogue all sources before treating any transcript as the whole trip. Note coverage gaps, non-speech action and the distinction between camera time and travel time. Never silently correct dates or move originals.

Keep a context ledger: fact, source, confidence, conflicting evidence, question. Use photo GPS and chosen booking messages to reconstruct places and travel order. A booking proves an intended itinerary, not that an event happened. Camera clocks and drone metadata may be wrong. Ask the author specific questions paired with short memory clips: what were you trying to do, what changed, what mattered, how did it end?

For each scene record location, setup, action, reaction/result, ending and transition. Review before/after the candidate interval. Preserve the full selected speech turn and a natural tail; camera-truncated speech should not be patched with invented words. Different expressive views can develop space, detail, beauty or atmosphere together. Reconsider an exhausted view without that function; do not reject every return to a location as a duplicate. Build the coverage map before selecting speech. Remove technical chatter, empty material and redundant takes within the brief; discuss removal of an entire meaningful story unless already authorized.

Narration adds personal context rather than describing everything visible. Mark uncertain lines as questions, collect the complete recording script and timing estimates, then retain the author's accepted takes. Never describe generated memories as autobiographical truth.

## Connect scenes into a film

Before adding a chapter, compare the trip map with used ranges and unshown events. A filename sequence or the latest status entry is not the story order. Record what the viewer already knows, why this scene follows, and what it hands to the next. Check callbacks and recurring jokes against the setup actually present in the film.

Name the function of each boundary: a new day, place change, omitted travel, continued action or return. Do not automatically give every chapter a full musical ending, black screen and fresh introduction. Preserve meaningful pauses and avoid implying continuous action across different days. Recheck the larger sequence when individual scenes feel disconnected; technical preservation alone does not prove narrative coherence.

Give a meaningful joke its setup, payoff and reaction. Restore a missing story as a whole unit instead of placing it between another scene's explanation and reveal. When the user asks for calmer pacing, distinguish trimming the searching tail of a shot from deleting the musical sequence that gives the scene room to breathe.
