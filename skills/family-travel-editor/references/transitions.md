# Motion transitions and spatial continuity

Use a transition for a particular change of place, attention or action. Available presets are not reasons to add one. A few motivated accents can coexist with straight cuts and deliberate chapter pauses; do not apply an effect to every cut or hide poor source selection with one.

## Identify the dominant camera gesture first

Motion is not limited to left and right. Classify the useful phase on both sides: pan, tilt, forward/backward travel, vertical travel, optical zoom, arc, or calm observation. Record subject motion separately. Small lateral sway during a forward walk is not a pan, and a passing vehicle is not evidence of camera direction.

For depth, examine expansion/contraction around the point of interest and landmarks at different distances. Parallax distinguishes camera travel from a flat scale change; an affine scale estimate alone cannot establish either. For vertical motion, distinguish a tilt from raising/lowering the camera and check actual screen movement. A weak component shared by two shots must not outweigh their dominant gestures.

Evaluate outgoing gesture → transition → incoming gesture, including phase, speed and attention. Forward motion in the first shot does not automatically justify a zoom transition into a side-facing second shot. A short, deliberate depth effect can be appropriate, but it is not permission to add digital zooms, freezes or retiming to repair poor source motion. Do not force one example of every direction into a sequence.

## Choose the connection before the preset

Compare a proposed effect with a straight-cut baseline using the same picture and sound.

| Intended connection | Candidate | Main check |
|---|---|---|
| Continue an action or guide attention | Cut on action; composition/eye-trace match | Preserve the useful action and align the viewer's point of interest; do not force a crop or invent continuity |
| Move between related views | Directional slide/pan or short depth accent | Both motion phases and the reveal support the gesture; avoid bounce-back and unreadable incoming shots |
| Pass behind a real foreground object | Occlusion cut or tracked mask | Actual footage supplies the cover; a generic foreground-wipe preset does not automatically track a person or doorway |
| Bridge a place or a conversation | J/L sound edit | Complete speech, authentic context and stable ambience; use [audio joins](audio-joins.md) |
| Indicate elapsed time or a chapter boundary | Dissolve or dip to black | The temporal break is intentional and not a substitute for missing coverage |
| Remove a small discontinuity within similar interview poses | Carefully tested Smooth Cut | Inspect faces, hands and geometry; it is not a general-purpose morph between unrelated locations |

For unfamiliar presets, inspect the installed implementation or controls and render a reversible sample. Names such as Pan, Zoom or Foreground Wipe do not establish what the effect actually does.

## Verify the direction of the effect itself

Distinguish physical camera direction from screen-space image movement. A rightward camera pan usually sends static scenery left. Preset names, streaks of blur and the fact that both neighboring clips have similar motion do not prove which way the rendered effect moves.

For each directional transition:

1. Inspect the last useful motion phase of the outgoing shot, every frame of the effect, and the first useful phase of the incoming shot, with enough context to detect a delayed reversal.
2. Track at least two readable stationary landmarks, such as a column, shelf edge or window frame. Follow the outgoing landmarks until they leave, then incoming landmarks until the next shot settles. Record screen-space direction before / during / after. Do not use a passing person, vehicle or reflection as the only reference.
3. For an effect intended to continue movement, avoid an unintended reversal, such as right → left → right, up → down → up, or expansion followed by contraction. A calm outgoing shot can introduce a directional accent if the incoming shot supports it. An intentional opposition needs a specific narrative purpose; do not invent that purpose after discovering a mismatch.
4. Check actual rendered frames, source handles, edges, geometry, faces, lettering and readability. Dense optical flow can support this review but may fail inside blur or over changing layers. Inspect trackable landmarks and the effect entry/exit instead of turning uncertain measurements into a pass.
5. Review the transition at normal playback speed with its surrounding action and music. Frame inspection, playback review and listening are separate evidence; report any unavailable part. If the effect remains distracting, adjust it or retain a good straight cut. Do not freeze, stretch or digitally reframe the source merely to make the effect fit.

Record export identity, cut time, preset/settings, landmark observations, intended effect, actual result and decision. No fixed duration or blur setting is universally correct. Compare a correction with the prior version and preserve protected material.

## Preserve the revealed action

The end of a sentence may be the beginning of a camera reveal. If someone describes a view, object or room, retain the useful reveal and enough time to understand it. A clean directional fragment can still be the wrong cut if it abandons that action. Check doorway → room → window or similar spatial relationships as a whole sequence.

Vary wide views, details, people, moving shots and naturally calmer live footage according to what the scene needs. Repeated corridor walks can be good individually yet monotonous in sequence. Do not solve that by deleting all movement or inserting artificial stills. Revisit [camera motion](camera-motion.md) for source selection and [preservation](preservation.md) for revisions.
