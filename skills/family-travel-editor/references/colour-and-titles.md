# Colour continuity and meaningful titles

## Trace the colour pipeline before grading

Identify the actual camera files, image profile, gamma, colour space and signal range. Inspect project colour management, input transforms, LUTs/CSTs and export settings. A camera model alone does not establish Log or HDR. Do not apply a transform twice; record an unknown profile as uncertain instead of guessing.

For stabilized or transcoded material, compare the same moment through the chain: camera original → derivative → editor before creative correction → corrected shot → actual export. This separates a capture problem from a processing error. Stabilization and a 4K file do not establish correct colour.

Match each shot with its neighbours for white balance, exposure, black/white points, contrast and saturation using both the image and scopes. Retain natural differences in weather and lighting. Identical settings do not guarantee matching cameras. Before applying a correction broadly, inspect representative sky, foliage, neutral surfaces and faces where present at a useful size; check clipping, crushed shadows and colour jumps at cuts. Record the inspected ranges and uncertainties.

## Decide what a title means before designing it

Check whether the story needs the title, whether place/time are supported, and which language and graphic style the filmmaker chose. Making a day change understandable does not automatically require a “next day” card. An internal scene label is not finished screen copy. Translating a confusing caption does not fix its purpose.

Review each changed title in the exported scene for wording, spelling, language, appearance time, face overlap, contrast, animation and reading time. Use the film's approved visual style; do not turn one family's preference for sparse titles into a universal ban.

Treat a route map as part of the trip's chronology. Place departure and arrival cues against the actual journey, and avoid announcing arrival then returning to unexplained en-route footage. Check route and place labels against sources, keep the moving route readable, and carry any arrival sound with the map when moving it. Map style and geographic detail follow the brief; invented geography is not a design shortcut.
